# Reader clarity

Use this during an edit of explanatory prose or when the brief asks for a check of reader understanding. It fits inside the existing editing pass. It does not require another agent, a score, or a report.

## Give the sentence enough room

A short passage can ask the reader to do too much reconstruction. Look for clipped clauses, dense strings of nouns, unexplained abbreviations, and symbols standing in for relationships. Restore the words needed to tell who acts, what changes, and when a condition applies.

Keep each relationship as precise as the material allows. If it states a reason, make that reason legible. If it only places two events together, preserve that limit. Adding "because" or "therefore" can introduce a claim even when every noun stays the same.

Keep a technical name that identifies the right thing. When the material supplies an explanation and the audience needs it, place that explanation near the name. Avoid replacing a precise term with a general word or defining vocabulary the intended reader already knows.

Some longer sentences carry a comparison or dependency more clearly than several short ones. Adjust their rhythm without removing qualifications or scattering the reasoning. Additional words should clarify existing information.

## Check what the reader can follow

For an explanatory passage, briefly work through it with the stated audience in mind:

- Describe its main claim in ordinary language, then check that the passage actually supports that reading.
- Follow the explanation from one sentence to the next. Locate any place where understanding depends on something present only in the brief or in the editor's head.
- For instructions, check that the reader can identify the action, its conditions, and any stated way to recognize completion.

Repair a break using information already available: move a supplied explanation, make a reference explicit, or reconnect clauses. A missing detail remains missing until the writer supplies it. Do not manufacture a reason, definition, or benefit to make the account feel complete.

Use this check in proportion to the passage. A novel can withhold a motive. Dialogue can be clipped. An essay can earn its argument gradually. Preserve those choices instead of forcing every piece to announce its conclusion immediately.

## Check the effect of the edit

Read the changed passage against its original meaning. Check whether a removed detail was limiting another statement, whether a simplified verb lost part of the action, or whether smoother wording changed uncertainty into a promise. Correct the wording rather than adding a new explanation around it.

Keep this reasoning private unless the user asks for editorial notes. Stop when the passage carries its meaning clearly in the intended voice.
