# Exemplars

Authors and books that exemplify the persona's voice. Use these as direction for the draft-implementer when the user gives no voice signature.

## Literary Fiction

| Author | Book | What to take |
|---|---|---|
| Marilynne Robinson | *Gilead* | long sentences as spoken meditation, no quotation marks for inner thought |
| Denis Johnson | *Jesus' Son* | fragments, hallucinatory specificity, dialogue as overheard radio |
| Cormac McCarthy | *The Road* | no quotation marks, brutal economy, em-dash discipline |
| Annie Proulx | *Brokeback Mountain* | verb-forward, regional vocabulary, no hedging |
| George Saunders | *Tenth of December* | voice that bends across stories, em-dashes used precisely |
| Toni Morrison | *Beloved* | sentence-level rhythm tied to spoken cadence |
| Mary Robison | *Why Did I Ever* | fragmented chapters, voice over plot |
| Ocean Vuong | *On Earth We're Briefly Gorgeous* | second-person, lyric, sentence as breath |

## Popular Nonfiction

| Author | Book | What to take |
|---|---|---|
| Michael Lewis | *The Big Short* | character-driven explanation through individual oddballs |
| Susan Orlean | *The Library Book* | concrete sensory detail per scene, refuses summary |
| John McPhee | *The Control of Nature* | sentence-by-sentence specificity about geology, weather, infrastructure |
| Ed Yong | *I Contain Multitudes* | scientific explanation that never lapses into encyclopedia voice |
| Patrick Radden Keefe | *Empire of Pain* | character introduction patterns, controlled em-dash use |
| Robert Caro | *The Power Broker* | scenes that anchor every claim in a real moment |
| Maggie Nelson | *Bluets* | fragment-as-paragraph, citation-heavy |
| Kathryn Schulz | *Being Wrong* | argument-as-essay, multiple registers per chapter |

## Technical Writing

| Author | Book | What to take |
|---|---|---|
| Donald Knuth | *The Art of Computer Programming* | terse, formal, unrelenting precision |
| Brian Kernighan & Dennis Ritchie | *The C Programming Language* | every sentence load-bearing |
| Martin Kleppmann | *Designing Data-Intensive Applications* | specific named systems, named tradeoffs |
| Robert Nystrom | *Crafting Interpreters* | voice that respects the reader, embedded humor that earns its place |
| Charles Petzold | *Code* | building from first principles, no padding |
| Mark Seemann | *Code That Fits in Your Head* | argument structure for engineering essays |
| Sandi Metz | *Practical Object-Oriented Design* | object-oriented thinking explained without analogy |

## Memoir

| Author | Book | What to take |
|---|---|---|
| Joan Didion | *The Year of Magical Thinking* | precise, repetitive, almost clinical voice of grief |
| Mary Karr | *The Liars' Club* | Texas vernacular, specific objects, no self-pity |
| Maggie Nelson | *The Argonauts* | fragmented, citation-heavy, refuses genre conventions |
| Ta-Nehisi Coates | *Between the World and Me* | second-person address, controlled rage, specific named places |
| Vivian Gornick | *Fierce Attachments* | mother-daughter scenes, walking-and-talking structure |
| Maxine Hong Kingston | *The Woman Warrior* | mythic register woven into autobiography |
| Roxane Gay | *Hunger* | physicality of memory, sentence-level honesty |
| James Baldwin | *Notes of a Native Son* | essayistic memoir, intellectual voice without distance |
| Edmund White | *A Boy's Own Story* | sensory specificity in childhood scenes |

## Business Book

| Author | Book | What to take |
|---|---|---|
| Ben Horowitz | *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* | first-person scenes from inside Loudcloud / Opsware, no synthesis |
| Phil Knight | *Shoe Dog* | memoir-of-business; specific scenes; refuses generic management voice |
| Brad Stone | *The Everything Store* | Amazon scenes with named executives, dates, specific decisions |
| Andrew Grove | *Only the Paranoid Survive* | strategic clarity, named tradeoffs, no synergy-speak |
| Patrick Radden Keefe | *Empire of Pain* | Sackler family / Purdue scene-building, voice that refuses corporate flattery |
| Liz Wiseman | *Multipliers* | concrete leader cases, refuses "key takeaways" boxing |
| Jim Collins | *Good to Great* | named-company analysis, non-formulaic chapter structures |
| Ron Chernow | *Titan* | biographical depth applied to business |

## Cross-Persona

Some exemplars work across multiple personas — Patrick Radden Keefe shows up in popular-nonfiction AND business-book, for example. That's because the voice (specific, scene-driven, journalistic) transfers. The voice-specialist should pull from these flexibly.
