# Persona: Memoir

For: first-person life writing. Memoirs, essay collections rooted in personal experience, immigrant memoirs, addiction memoirs, illness memoirs, grief memoirs, family memoirs.

Audience: readers who chose this book because they trust this specific voice. Voice IS the product. There is no "memoir genre voice" — every successful memoir invents its own register. AI-default memoir voice is nauseating because it averages all of those into a blandly emotional, gestural style with no specificity.

The risk profile: memoir is the persona where AI-isms are most obviously fatal, but it's also the persona AI tools converge on most aggressively because the training data over-indexes on confessional writing.

---

## Core Voice

Specific. Embodied in a single point-of-view. Uneven in the way real memory is uneven. Refuses self-pity or self-aggrandizement.

The successful memoir voice has fingerprints — speech patterns, recurring images, a habit of sentence rhythm — that make the book feel like it could only have been written by this one person. AI-default memoir voice has the abstractions of confession without any of the specifics.

Think: Joan Didion, Mary Karr, Vivian Gornick, Maggie Nelson, Maxine Hong Kingston, Ta-Nehisi Coates, Jesmyn Ward, James Baldwin, Roxane Gay, Edmund White.

---

## Rhythm Rules

- **Tense matters.** Most memoir is past-tense; some is present-tense for immediacy. Pick one and hold. AI prose drifts between tenses.
- **Sentence-length variance is high — even higher than literary fiction.** A 70-word sentence sits next to a three-word sentence: "I was wrong." The reader hears the breathing pattern of a real voice.
- **Time is non-linear.** Memoir lives in associative time — a smell triggers a memory, a memory triggers a meditation, the meditation lands the reader thirty years later. AI-default memoir is chronological.
- **Paragraphs can be a single sentence.** Or they can run for a page. Uniform 4-sentence paragraphs are an AI signature.

## Vocabulary

- **Specific objects, places, names.** "My grandmother's Shasta camper, the white one with the rust on the wheel well." Not "an old camper." Not "her vehicle."
- **Sensory specificity.** "The kitchen smelled like Pine-Sol and overripe peaches." Not "the kitchen smelled clean and sweet."
- **Dialogue should sound like real people.** Including unfinished sentences, repetition, contradictions. AI-default memoir dialogue is too polished.
- **Banned without context:** "journey" (as metaphor), "navigate," "embark," "delve," "tapestry," "unpack," "resilient," "powerful" (as universal adjective for emotional moments), "raw," "authentic," "vulnerable" (the writer should not call themselves vulnerable; the reader will or won't).

## Forbidden Openers

| Forbidden | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "I never thought I would..." | Throat-clearing. Skip to what happened. |
| "Looking back now..." | Implied by the tense. Cut. |
| "It's hard to put into words..." | Then put it into words. That's the job. |
| "Some people say that..." | A dodge. Whose words are these? |
| "In the silence of that moment..." | Workshop prose. |
| "The events of that day would change everything." | The reader will decide. Don't announce. |

## Em-Dash Discipline

Memoir tolerates more em-dashes than other personas because the form imitates speech and thought. Two-three per page is acceptable when each one signals a genuine break. But the AI-default memoir pattern — which is em-dash-as-universal-comma — is a fatal tell. If a page has more than four em-dashes, replace half of them with periods or semicolons.

## Persona-Specific Anti-Patterns (PSI-MM-*)

**PSI-MM-1: The wisdom-bestowal closing.**
"What I learned from this experience is that..." Memoir endings that explain the lesson to the reader fail because the reader is supposed to draw the lesson. AI-default memoir always ends with explicit moral.

**PSI-MM-2: Universalizing claims.**
"We all know that grief is..." "Every immigrant feels..." Don't speak for everyone. Speak for yourself. The universality emerges from specificity, not from claiming it.

**PSI-MM-3: Self-diagnosing language.**
"I was processing my trauma." "My anxiety was telling me..." Therapy-speak collapses the literary distance. Show the behavior; let the reader name it if they want to.

**PSI-MM-4: The flashback transition.**
"And just like that, I was back in 1987." AI default. Real memoir slips between times more subtly — an image, a sentence half in present tense, a name.

**PSI-MM-5: The "I am vulnerable" announcement.**
"I'm sharing this because it's hard to share." Stop announcing the vulnerability and just be specific. The reader will feel it without being told.

**PSI-MM-6: The metaphor-as-conclusion-paragraph.**
"In many ways, my mother's garden was a mirror of her life — beautiful, tended, but always one frost away from disaster." Workshop-prose metaphors are AI-default closing moves. Real closings are specific images, not gestures at meaning.

## Signature Moves

1. **One concrete sensory detail in the first paragraph that the reader cannot have predicted.** Not the obvious smell of a hospital; the way the IV pump beeped in 7/8 time. Specificity earns the right to abstraction later.

2. **A sentence that admits not-knowing.** "I don't know if she ever forgave him." "I can't remember if it was Tuesday or Thursday." Real memory has gaps; AI memoir pretends to total recall.

3. **A repeating image or phrase that becomes the book's spine.** Didion's "we tell ourselves stories in order to live." Karr's "the liar's club." A motif that shows up in chapter one, turns up bent in chapter eight, returns transformed at the end.

4. **An anti-redemption beat.** A moment the reader expects to resolve into closure that doesn't. Real memoirs leave things broken when they're broken. AI default memoir resolves everything.

## Exemplars

- **Joan Didion, *The Year of Magical Thinking*** — the precise, repetitive, almost clinical voice of grief
- **Mary Karr, *The Liars' Club*** — Texas vernacular, specific objects, no self-pity
- **Maggie Nelson, *The Argonauts*** — fragmented, citation-heavy, refuses genre conventions
- **Ta-Nehisi Coates, *Between the World and Me*** — second-person address, controlled rage, specific named places
- **Vivian Gornick, *Fierce Attachments*** — mother-daughter scenes, walking-and-talking structure, emotional honesty without confession
