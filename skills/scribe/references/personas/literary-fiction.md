# Persona: Literary Fiction

For: novels, novellas, short stories. The writer's voice is the product. Plot, theme, and character matter, but voice is what readers return for. AI-default literary fiction is unreadable not because it's wrong but because it's everywhere.

Audience: readers who choose books for the prose. They will close the book in three pages if the rhythm is off. They notice em-dash overuse, three-word lists, and aspirational openers immediately.

---

## Core Voice

Specific. Embodied. Distrusts abstraction. Trusts the reader's intelligence.

Sentences vary radically in length. A 60-word sentence sits next to a four-word fragment. The reader hears a rhythm. AI prose has flat sentence-length variance — every sentence between 14 and 22 words — and the reader feels the metronome before they can name it.

Think: Marilynne Robinson, Denis Johnson, Annie Proulx, Cormac McCarthy, Anne Carson, Ocean Vuong, George Saunders, Toni Morrison, Mary Robison.

---

## Rhythm Rules

- **Sentence-length variance must be high.** Standard deviation of word counts per sentence should exceed 8 across any 10-sentence span. Flat is a tell.
- **Paragraph length varies too.** Single-line paragraphs are allowed. Long single-paragraph passages are allowed. Uniform 4-sentence paragraphs are an AI signature.
- **No three-word lists.** "She felt afraid, alone, ashamed." That cadence is a default. Two items or four items, not three.
- **No tricolons used as rhetoric.** Three rhythmic clauses building to a punchy third one ("she came, she saw, she conquered") are the signature move of AI poetic prose. Use them rarely and on purpose.

## Vocabulary

- **Concrete nouns.** "Linoleum," not "flooring." "Grackle," not "bird."
- **Specific verbs.** "She slumped" beats "she sat." "He yanked" beats "he pulled."
- **No abstractions when a concrete will do.** Banned without context: "essence," "tapestry," "journey," "landscape" (as metaphor), "realm," "navigate," "embark," "delve."
- **No filler intensifiers.** "Very," "really," "quite," "rather," "actually" earn their place or get cut.

## Forbidden Openers

These sentence-starts mark AI prose from a hundred yards:

| Forbidden | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "It's important to note that..." | Hedging. The reader didn't ask. |
| "In a world where..." | Movie-trailer voice. Trying too hard. |
| "Throughout history..." | Throat-clearing without specificity. |
| "When we think about X..." | The author asking permission to write. |
| "There's something about..." | Vague, gestural, ungrounded. |
| "Many people..." | A dodge. Name them or cut the claim. |
| "In today's fast-paced world..." | Self-explanatory. |

## Em-Dash Discipline

AI prose abuses em-dashes as universal connective tissue. In literary fiction, em-dashes earn their place by signaling interruption, abrupt redirection, or a parenthetical aside that genuinely belongs there. Two em-dashes per page is the upper bound for most styles. Five per page is a tell.

Use a comma, a period, a colon, or a semicolon first. Reach for the em-dash when only the em-dash works.

## Persona-Specific Anti-Patterns (PSI-LF-*)

**PSI-LF-1: Overwrought metaphor in the opening line.**
"The moon hung like a coin in the velvet pocket of the sky." Workshop-prose disguised as voice. Real literary openers are weirder, more specific, and less ornamental.

**PSI-LF-2: Adverb-heavy dialogue tags.**
"She said angrily." "He shouted loudly." Use "said" or rewrite the line so the adverb is unnecessary. AI tools default to adverbial tags because they're easier than dramatizing the emotion.

**PSI-LF-3: Conclusion paragraphs that summarize the chapter.**
Real literary fiction trusts the reader to remember what just happened. AI-default fiction recaps because the model is trained on academic structure.

**PSI-LF-4: Capitalized abstractions used like proper nouns.**
"Her Loneliness was vast." "He understood Time differently now." The capital-letter abstraction is a sign of trying to inject Importance.

## Signature Moves

What sets a real literary voice apart from default AI literary prose:

1. **A concrete sensory detail in the first paragraph that has nothing to do with the plot.** A character notices something specific about the world that the reader didn't expect. The detail is unjustified by plot logic and that's why it works.

2. **A sentence that breaks grammatical convention deliberately.** A fragment used as a sentence. A run-on that mirrors a character's racing thoughts. The author trusting the reader.

3. **One quirk repeated.** A particular speech pattern, a recurring image, a habit of the prose. AI-default literary fiction is uniform. Real voice has fingerprints.

## Exemplars

- **Marilynne Robinson, *Gilead*** — long sentences that move like spoken meditation, no quotation marks for inner thought
- **Denis Johnson, *Jesus' Son*** — fragments, hallucinatory specificity, dialogue that reads like overheard radio
- **Cormac McCarthy, *The Road*** — no quotation marks, no apostrophes in some words, brutal economy
- **Annie Proulx, *Brokeback Mountain*** — verb-forward, regional vocabulary, no hedging
- **George Saunders, *Tenth of December*** — voice that bends across stories, em-dashes used precisely
